We got just a taste of that seemingly next-generation model that could take over the world. Or so it was hyped. I took advantage of our ~5 days with Claude Fable to build a few things. It was great, did what I needed it to, and I maxed out my usage along the way. But the time I spent with this model led me to believe in a totally different direction. The frontier labs have the ability to rip away the greatness from us whenever they choose. We all got rug-pulled at an insane scale. So now it's time to take control of the direction myself.

For the last few months I could sense there was going to be a day when the labs decided to no longer subsidize our lives with the agents. The numbers coming out are ridiculous. OpenAI losing billions of dollars; SemiAnalysis bought every paid tier from OpenAI and Anthropic, ran each one into the ground, and priced the tokens at standard API rates — a $200 Claude Max plan can represent up to $8,000 of monthly compute (ChatGPT Pro came out closer to $14,000). That's the maxed-out number, not what the median user pulls, but it's exactly the heavy power user the labs are bleeding on. This isn't sustainable, and it will almost certainly go away as soon as these organizations IPO and are dealing with real shareholders instead of investor hype.

<sub>Source: [SemiAnalysis on X](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2064815044085318040) — bought each paid tier, maxed out usage, and priced the tokens at standard API rates.</sub>

Even at the organizational level, we see companies getting close to a 1:1 ratio of salary cost to token cost. That is just not a sustainable strategy. At some point companies are going to ask, "How do we do this without affecting our bottom line?" — and there will need to be a real answer.

We saw similar financial models with so many pre-IPO companies that always took a loss. Uber, WeWork, DoorDash, Spotify — all were hemorrhaging cash while culturally absorbing users. It's the cycle of startups: earn off of hype, IPO, then start charging more to get into the black. It is coming, and anyone who believes it isn't is just being naive.

So I'm deciding not to wait to lose the ability to work with agents. I'm jumping head first into owning my own pipelines, learning how these models work and behave, and recreating the value I find with AI by building it for myself. I've picked up a DGX Spark, and over the next while I'll be dropping content about what I build, how I do it, the wins, the losses, and all of the fun learnings along the way.

This age of AI is not going anywhere any time soon, so why not figure it out for ourselves? Follow along on this journey. I might make a fool of myself, I might teach you a thing or two. Regardless of what happens, I think I'll be learning a lot — and that is what matters most to me.

Some day, I hope my words will help some organization make a better decision on how to handle using AI.
