When someone buys NBA League Pass and presses play, the game either starts or it doesn't. Behind that press sits a chain: an account, a purchase, and a system deciding this person gets this game. I joined the NBA's NextGen rewrite working on that chain, from signing in and paying through to watching.

Then came Local Access, which lets fans stream their local team's games in their own market. Each team was its own launch with its own stakeholders, and my scope stopped being a slice of the chain and became the launches themselves.

By the end my work had moved up a level, from the games to the pages that sold them. I led the build of a system that rendered package pages out of Gutenberg blocks, the editor system WordPress uses, so one composed page could serve the whole subscription lineup.

I owned my piece of the chain, then my launches, and the next thing I wanted was the version where every decision was mine, including the wrong ones.
