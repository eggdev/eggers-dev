# Product

## Register

The site speaks as a senior practitioner talking to a peer: plain declaratives, concrete nouns, and no adjective doing work the evidence should do. It states what was built and what happened, then stops — the brevity reads as confidence, not thinness. Where there is nothing specific to say, it says less rather than reaching for enthusiasm.

## Users

**Primary: recruiters and hiring managers.** They arrive while evaluating the owner for a role, often skimming many candidates back-to-back, frequently on a phone, between meetings, with little time and healthy skepticism. In under a minute they need to answer three questions: *What does this person do? Is the work actually good? How do I reach them?*

**Secondary: peers and potential collaborators** who follow a link and form a fast credibility judgment.

Job to be done: in under a minute, understand who the owner is and what they can do, see proof of it, and reach out (or remember the page) without bouncing.

## Product Purpose

eggers.dev is the personal portfolio of a multi-disciplinary builder working across **design and engineering**. It exists to make a strong, fast first impression on people deciding whether to hire or work with them, present a focused selection of work as evidence, and lower the friction to making contact.

Success: a recruiter lands, immediately grasps what the owner does, believes the work is strong, and reaches out — without a second of confusion about who this is or how to get in touch.

## Brand Personality

Confident and polished. The voice is **direct, specific, and unembellished** — a senior practitioner who lets the work carry the argument rather than leaning on adjectives. Three words: **confident, precise, considered.**

Because the owner spans design and engineering, the site itself should read as the work of someone fluent in both craft and systems — the page is a sample of the work, not just a description of it. Emotional goal: a visitor leaves feeling they've met someone who is clearly good at what they do and easy to work with.

## Anti-references

What eggers.dev must **not** look like:

- **The generic developer-portfolio template** — dark purple-gradient hero, "Hi, I'm a passionate developer," skill-percentage bars, a wall of identical project cards.
- **Corporate / SaaS marketing aesthetics** — hero-metric blocks, feature-icon grids, gradient buttons, stock-illustration filler.
- **Over-animated showcases** — scroll-jacking, parallax for its own sake, heavy WebGL, any motion that fights the user. Motion stays minimal and never overrides native scroll.
- **Clutter / maximalism** — elements competing for attention, no clear hierarchy, no breathing room.
- **The saturated AI editorial-magazine lane** specifically — display-serif italic headline + tiny tracked mono eyebrows + ruled three-column separators + monochrome-by-reflex. The owner wants editorial *confidence*; deliver it through bold typographic hierarchy and spatial composition, not that cliché fingerprint.

Reference north stars (the *feel*, not to copy): **rauno.me** (refined restraint, interaction craft, system-like calm), **brittanychiang.com** (clean, content-forward, effortless to navigate), **p5aholic.me** (creative-coder polish). The throughline across all three: minimal but never generic, fast, focused, clean.

## Design Principles

1. **Work over words.** The strongest claim is the work itself. Show proof before describing it; let the projects argue the case.
2. **Respect the visitor's time.** A recruiter should get the gist in under a minute. Clarity and speed beat depth-on-arrival; contact should never be more than a glance away.
3. **Confidence through restraint.** Minimal, not empty — and distinct, not generic. Every element earns its place; the polish *is* the differentiator. Restraint here means precision, not absence.
4. **Bold type, quiet motion.** Carry the visual confidence with typographic hierarchy and composition. Keep motion to clean, fast transitions into content; never hijack the scroll or animate for spectacle.
5. **The page is the proof.** The site should read as made by someone who designs *and* builds. Its craft, performance, and finish are themselves evidence of the owner's ability.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, placeholder text held to the same body-text bar. Full keyboard navigation with clearly visible focus states. Every animation ships a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback (crossfade or instant) — given the low-motion brief, this is a short step from the default. Semantic HTML with proper landmarks for screen readers. Fast on mobile and low-end devices: for a skim-and-bounce recruiter audience, performance is an accessibility concern, not just a nicety.
